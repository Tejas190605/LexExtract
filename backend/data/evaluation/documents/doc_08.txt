FINAL ORDER
1. The Complainant booked a residential 3-BHK apartment in the project 'Skyline Heights' developed by the Opposite Party in Bavdhan, Pune, and paid a total consideration of Rs. 92,50,000 between June 2020 and December 2022.

2. The agreement for sale stipulated delivery of physical vacant possession on or before 31st December 2023 with a grace period of six months. The Opposite Party failed to obtain the Occupancy Certificate and complete civil amenities, committing gross deficiency in service under Section 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act 2019.

3. The Complainant seeks full refund of the amount deposited along with delayed interest and compensation for mental agony under Section 47 of the Consumer Protection Act 2019.

4. The Opposite Party contested the complaint, pleading force majeure due to supply chain disruption and delay in sanction of drainage approvals by municipal authorities.

5. We find that the defence of force majeure is unsubstantiated. An allottee cannot be made to wait indefinitely for possession of an apartment. Following the law laid down by the Supreme Court of India in Experion Developers Pvt Ltd v. Sushma Ashok Shiroor:

ORDER:
(a) The Opposite Party is directed to refund the entire amount of Rs. 92,50,000 to the Complainant along with interest at 9% per annum from the date of each deposit until realization.
(b) The Opposite Party shall further pay Rs. 1,00,000 towards compensation for mental harassment and Rs. 50,000 towards litigation costs within 45 days.
