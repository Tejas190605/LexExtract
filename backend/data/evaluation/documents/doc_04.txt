IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
CRIMINAL APPELLATE JURISDICTION
Criminal Appeal No. 782 of 2025

Rameshwar Yadav                                   ... Appellant
VERSUS
State of Uttar Pradesh                           ... Respondent

ORDER
1. This appeal by special leave under Article 136 of the Constitution of India arises out of the final judgment and order passed by the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad in Criminal Appeal No. 1104 of 2020, affirming the conviction of the Appellant under Section 302 read with Section 34 of the Indian Penal Code 1860.

2. The prosecution case rests on circumstantial evidence concerning an incident alleged to have taken place on 4th August 2018 in District Varanasi. The trial court sentenced the Appellant to undergo rigorous imprisonment for life and imposed a fine of Rs. 25,000.

3. Mr. Kapil Sibal, learned Senior Counsel appearing for the Appellant, submitted that there are material contradictions in the testimonies of PW-1 and PW-2. It is further argued that the chain of circumstantial evidence is broken, and the recovery of the alleged weapon does not satisfy the requirements of Section 27 of the Indian Evidence Act 1872.

4. Ms. Aishwarya Bhati, Additional Solicitor General for the State of Uttar Pradesh, supported the concurrent findings of conviction, submitting that motive and last seen theory stand proved beyond reasonable doubt.

5. We have considered the rival submissions and scrutinized the trial record. It is a well-settled principle of criminal jurisprudence that suspicion, however strong, cannot take the place of legal proof. The circumstantial evidence fails to satisfy the panoply of five golden principles laid down in Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra.

6. Consequently, the conviction and sentence are set aside. The Appellant is acquitted of all charges and directed to be released forthwith, unless required in any other case.
